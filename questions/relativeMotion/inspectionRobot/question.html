A small inspection robot drives on a wide conveyor belt that moves east. A camera attached to the factory ceiling records these two trials:

  • When the robot drives toward the belt's eastern end, the camera measures its speed as {{params.east_trial}} m/s east.
  • When the robot reverses and drives toward the western end, the camera measures its speed as {{params.west_trial}} m/s west.

The belt runs at a constant speed. The robot maintains the same speed relative to the belt in every direction. Consider only the steady motion during each trial, after any turn is complete.

Round speeds to the nearest 0.01 m/s and angles to the nearest 0.1°. Keep unrounded values during calculations.

1. Determine the two speeds

What are the robot's speed relative to the belt and the belt's speed relative to the factory floor, respectively?

Robot: {{params.robot_speed}} m/s; belt: {{params.belt_speed}} m/s Robot: {{params.east_trial}} m/s; belt: {{params.west_trial}} m/s Robot: {{params.belt_speed}} m/s; belt: {{params.robot_speed}} m/s Robot: {{params.twice_robot_speed}} m/s; belt: {{params.twice_belt_speed}} m/s

2. Follow the line on the floor

A red line painted on the factory floor runs north–south. The operator wants the camera to show the robot moving north along this line, with no eastward or westward motion. Which direction should the robot point, and what speed will the camera measure?

Angles below are measured starting from north and turning toward the indicated direction.

Aim {{params.compensation_angle}}° toward west; camera measures {{params.north_speed}} m/s Aim {{params.compensation_angle}}° toward east; camera measures {{params.north_speed}} m/s Aim {{params.observed_angle}}° toward west; camera measures {{params.robot_speed}} m/s Aim {{params.complement_angle}}° toward west; camera measures {{params.north_speed}} m/s

3. Point the robot north

In a separate trial, the operator points the robot exactly north relative to the belt. The robot and belt retain their original speeds. What motion does the ceiling camera now record?

{{params.observed_speed}} m/s, directed {{params.observed_angle}}° from north toward east {{params.north_speed}} m/s, directed {{params.compensation_angle}}° from north toward west {{params.robot_speed}} m/s, directed exactly north {{params.scalar_sum}} m/s, directed {{params.observed_angle}}° from north toward east

Worked solution

Let $u$ be the robot's speed relative to the belt and $b$ the belt's speed relative to the floor. Taking east as positive, the first observation gives $u+b={{params.east_trial}}$. The westward trial gives $-u+b=-{{params.west_trial}}$, or $u-b={{params.west_trial}}$.

Adding and subtracting the equations gives \[ u=\frac{ {{params.east_trial}}+{{params.west_trial}} }{2} ={{params.robot_speed}}\ \mathrm{m/s},\qquad b=\frac{ {{params.east_trial}}-{{params.west_trial}} }{2} ={{params.belt_speed}}\ \mathrm{m/s}. \]

To follow the red line, the robot's westward component must cancel the belt's eastward velocity. For an angle $\alpha$ measured from north toward west, $u\sin\alpha=b$. Thus \[ \alpha=\sin^{-1}(b/u)={{params.compensation_angle}}^\circ, \qquad v_{\mathrm{north}}=u\cos\alpha=\sqrt{u^2-b^2} ={{params.north_speed}}\ \mathrm{m/s}. \] This angle describes where the robot points; the camera sees motion exactly north.

When the robot instead points north relative to the belt, the camera sees an eastward velocity component $b$ and a northward component $u$: \[ v=\sqrt{u^2+b^2}={{params.observed_speed}}\ \mathrm{m/s}, \qquad \theta=\tan^{-1}(b/u)={{params.observed_angle}}^\circ. \] The observed path points ${{params.observed_angle}}^\circ$ from north toward east.
